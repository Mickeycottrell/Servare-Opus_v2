title: "How a Fractional EA Can Give You Back 10 Hours a Week"
description: "A breakdown of where founder time actually goes, and what disappears first when you bring in real support."
pubDate: 2026-08-01
---

When we start with a new client, the first thing we do is a time audit — a week of tracking exactly where the hours go. The results are almost always the same: a surprising share of a CEO's week is spent on work that doesn't require a CEO.

**Where the hours typically go**

Calendar management and rescheduling: 3-4 hours a week. Inbox triage and drafting routine responses: 2-3 hours. Travel planning and itinerary building: 1-2 hours. Chasing vendors, tracking action items, and general follow-up: 2-3 hours.

Added up, that's 8 to 12 hours a week — roughly a full working day — spent on tasks that a senior executive assistant can handle end-to-end.

**What changes first**

Clients usually notice the calendar first. Within the first two weeks, meetings stop colliding, buffer time reappears between calls, and the "let me check and get back to you" emails disappear. Travel and board prep tend to smooth out over the following month, once the EA has enough context to anticipate what's needed rather than just react to requests.

**The real return**

The hours matter, but the bigger shift is usually mental — fewer open loops, fewer things half-tracked in your own head. That's harder to measure, but it's the thing most clients mention first when we ask what's changed.
