---
title: "Why More CEOs Are Sharing an Executive Assistant"
description: "The fractional EA model is catching on with founders who need senior-level support without a full-time hire."
pubDate: 2026-07-10
---

For years, the executive assistant hire followed a predictable pattern: a founder gets busy enough to justify the headcount, posts a job, and spends months searching for someone senior enough to trust with a calendar, an inbox, and increasingly sensitive logistics. By the time the hire is made, the founder has usually been underwater for a year.

The fractional model flips that timeline. Instead of one EA dedicated entirely to one CEO, an experienced assistant works with a small, deliberately matched group of founders — each getting real, senior-level attention for the hours they actually need.

**Why it works**

Most CEOs don't have 40 hours a week of executive-assistant work. They have 10 to 20 hours of high-value work — calendar triage, travel logistics, board prep — surrounded by a lot of idle time that a full-time hire would otherwise be paid for. A fractional EA closes that gap: you get someone experienced enough to work independently, at a cost that matches the actual workload.

**What it isn't**

A fractional EA is not a task-rabbit-style assistant picking up ad hoc requests with no context on your business. The model only works if the EA is genuinely embedded — knows your calendar rhythms, your travel preferences, and how you like decisions escalated. That continuity is the whole point.

If your week is full of scheduling conflicts and logistics that don't require your judgment, it might be time to look at shared executive support instead of waiting for the "right time" to make a full-time hire.
